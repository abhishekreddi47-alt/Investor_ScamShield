Common Digital Investment Scams & Safety Rules

Understand the deceptive psychology used by digital fraudsters before you commit hard-earned savings.

🚨

Already Transferred Money? Act Within the "Golden Hour"

If you have sent funds to an unknown individual or fraudulent scheme, the first 60–120 minutes are critical to freeze interbank transfers before fraudsters siphon money into cash mules or crypto.

Step 1: Dial 1930 Immediately Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline (Ministry of Home Affairs). Provide transaction UTR, date, recipient UPI/bank account, and amount.
Step 2: Alert Your Bank Call your bank's fraud control unit to lodge a payment dispute and request a stop payment / freeze memo.
Step 3: File Formal Complaint Submit the official digital cyber complaint at cybercrime.gov.in and keep the acknowledgment number.

6 Common Scam Archetypes Targeting Emerging Investors

📈

1. The "Guaranteed Return / Double Money" Scheme

HIGH-RISK WARNING SIGN: Assured Daily/Weekly Profit

How it works: The message promises 30% to 100% returns within days or weeks with "zero risk." Often, fraudsters show fabricated ledger screenshots or even credit a nominal initial amount (₹200 to ₹1,000) to win trust, before encouraging large transfers of ₹50,000 or ₹5,00,000.

The Golden Truth: In legitimate financial markets, no instrument—equity, mutual fund, debt, or bond—can lawfully guarantee high fixed returns. High return mathematically implies high market risk.

📱

2. The Telegram / WhatsApp "VIP Stock Tips" Trap

HIGH-RISK WARNING SIGN: Pump-and-Dump Social Proof

How it works: Users are added without consent to Telegram channels or WhatsApp communities. Accomplice accounts post constant fake thank-you messages, luxury car photos, and photoshopped Zerodha/Groww P&L screenshots.

The Golden Truth: Fraudsters purchase illiquid penny stocks in advance, urge group members to buy at inflated prices, and then dump their holdings, causing the stock to crash and leaving retail investors with total losses.

🏛️

3. The Counterfeit SEBI / RBI Certificate Scam

HIGH-RISK WARNING SIGN: Unverified Regulatory Credentials

How it works: Scammers send high-resolution images of "SEBI Registration Certificates" or "RBI Approvals" bearing official emblems, fake registration numbers (e.g. INH00000XXXX), and forged signatures.

The Golden Truth: Never rely on image documents or registration numbers sent directly by the party. Always independently search the registered entity on sebi.gov.in and cross-verify the official listed telephone number and email domain.

💻

4. Remote Access Installation (AnyDesk / TeamViewer)

CRITICAL DANGER: Total Device Takeover

How it works: The caller or chatter claims to assist you in "opening a VIP institutional trading account" or "resolving KYC verification" and asks you to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or QuickSupport and read out a 9-digit session code.

The Golden Truth: Sharing that code grants full remote control of your phone or laptop. Scammers can see your screen, observe you entering banking passwords, and silently intercept SMS OTPs.

🎬

5. Part-Time Job / Video Like / Review Rating Trap

HIGH-RISK WARNING SIGN: Task Scam Escalation

How it works: Initiated via SMS/WhatsApp offering ₹50 per YouTube like or Google review. After paying ₹200–₹500 for initial tasks, the operator redirects the victim to a "prepaid cryptocurrency/investment task" where you must deposit ₹10,000 to "unlock" ₹15,000 earnings.

The Golden Truth: The victim is never allowed to withdraw their deposit; additional "tax fees" and "clearance fees" are continually demanded until the victim realizes the deception.

⚖️

6. "Digital Arrest" & Fake Law Enforcement Coercion

CRITICAL DANGER: Intimidation & Video Call Isolation

How it works: Scammers pose as Mumbai Police, CBI, or Customs officers over Skype/WhatsApp video calls wearing uniforms. They claim your Aadhaar or bank account was implicated in money laundering and instruct you to transfer funds to a "Supreme Court verification account."

The Golden Truth: There is legally NO such procedure as a "digital arrest" under Indian law. Indian police and judicial agencies never demand fund transfers for verification over video calls.
